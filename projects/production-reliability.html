Executive Summary

Production systems occasionally fail in ways that cannot be reproduced in development or test environments. This project focused on investigating an intermittent production issue affecting a customer-facing API where requests would occasionally fail because of temporary downstream response delays.

Rather than introducing broad architectural changes, I investigated the failure pattern using structured logging and Splunk, determined that the issue resulted from transient—not permanent—communication failures, and implemented a targeted retry strategy using Resilience4j. The resulting solution improved application resilience while preserving existing business behaviour and minimizing delivery risk.

Business Context

The backend service formed part of a life insurance platform responsible for serving customer-facing requests that depended on multiple enterprise systems, including policy, member, and account services.

Reliable communication between these systems was essential because failures directly affected customer journeys and generated additional work for support and operational teams. Maintaining high availability therefore required both robust application design and efficient production incident investigation.

Production Incident

An API began failing intermittently while communicating with one of its downstream enterprise services. The failures appeared inconsistent and could not be reproduced reliably in lower environments, making traditional debugging approaches ineffective.

Because the issue occurred only under production conditions, engineering decisions needed to be based on production evidence rather than assumptions. Any proposed solution also needed to avoid introducing unnecessary risk by changing business logic or customer-facing API behaviour.

The challenge was therefore twofold:

  • Identify the actual failure pattern without disrupting production traffic.
  • Implement a safe recovery mechanism only after confirming that the failures were genuinely transient.

My Engineering Ownership

I was responsible for investigating the production failures, identifying the underlying failure pattern, evaluating appropriate resilience strategies, and implementing a production-safe solution that improved reliability without changing business behaviour or introducing unnecessary operational risk.

Rather than treating the issue as an isolated exception, I approached it as a production reliability problem. The objective was to understand why failures occurred, determine whether recovery could be automated safely, and validate that any solution improved customer experience without negatively affecting downstream systems.

My responsibilities included:

  • Analyzing production logs to identify common characteristics across failed requests.
  • Comparing successful and failed execution paths to isolate the point of failure.
  • Determining whether failures were transient or permanent before selecting a recovery strategy.
  • Implementing a controlled retry mechanism using Resilience4j.
  • Validating production behaviour after deployment through continued monitoring.
  • Collaborating with the team during investigation, testing, deployment, and post-release verification.

Investigation Process

Because the issue could not be reproduced consistently outside production, application logs became the primary source of evidence. Instead of relying on assumptions, I analyzed structured JSON logs within Splunk to reconstruct the lifecycle of affected requests.

Reviewing timestamps, request context, exception details, and downstream responses revealed that failures consistently occurred while waiting for a particular dependency. Comparing successful and unsuccessful requests showed that the downstream service eventually recovered without manual intervention, indicating a transient communication issue rather than a permanent application failure.

The investigation followed a structured process:

  1. Collect production evidence from structured application logs.
  2. Compare successful and failed request executions.
  3. Identify recurring failure patterns and affected dependencies.
  4. Determine whether failures represented transient or permanent conditions.
  5. Select the least risky engineering solution based on observed behaviour.
  6. Validate the solution through testing and production monitoring.

Reliability Strategy

The investigation demonstrated that requests generally succeeded if the downstream dependency had slightly more time to recover. This made a controlled retry strategy more appropriate than attempting to redesign the application or introduce unnecessary complexity.

Instead of modifying business workflows, the solution focused on improving communication reliability between services. Recoverable failures were given a limited opportunity to succeed automatically, reducing customer-visible errors without affecting successful requests.

This approach improved resilience while keeping the implementation focused, predictable, and suitable for the observed production behaviour.

Key Engineering Decisions

Evidence before implementation

Production incidents often generate many possible explanations. Rather than making speculative code changes, engineering decisions were based entirely on observable production data collected through structured logging and Splunk analysis.

Retry only transient failures

Retries are beneficial only when failures are temporary. Applying retries to permanent failures increases latency, consumes additional resources, and may create duplicate processing. The investigation confirmed that the observed timeouts were transient, making retry an appropriate recovery mechanism.

Controlled retry behaviour

The retry policy was intentionally conservative. Configuration balanced recovery against the risk of placing unnecessary load on downstream services, ensuring that retries improved reliability without creating additional operational problems.

Avoid unnecessary complexity

Although resilience patterns such as circuit breakers provide additional protection, the observed production behaviour did not justify introducing them immediately. A targeted retry strategy addressed the actual failure mode while keeping the solution easier to understand, validate, and maintain.

Production Validation

Following implementation, the application was monitored to verify that requests recovered successfully from transient downstream delays while continuing to behave correctly under normal operating conditions.

Structured logging remained central to this validation process. Production logs confirmed that retries occurred only when expected, successful requests continued unaffected, and the application maintained consistent business behaviour after deployment.

Lessons Learned

This project reinforced that production reliability begins with observability. Without high-quality logs and measurable evidence, even experienced engineers can spend significant time pursuing incorrect assumptions. The fastest path to resolution is often understanding the failure before attempting to eliminate it.

Several engineering principles proved especially valuable:

  • Investigate production incidents using evidence rather than assumptions.
  • Differentiate transient failures from permanent failures before selecting a recovery strategy.
  • Apply resilience patterns selectively based on observed system behaviour rather than using them by default.
  • Invest in structured logging because production debugging depends on the quality of available operational data.
  • Prioritize simple, well-targeted solutions over introducing unnecessary architectural complexity.

What I Would Improve Today

If I were evolving the same service today, I would strengthen its observability and resilience capabilities while maintaining the same principle of introducing complexity only where it provides measurable value.

  • Introduce OpenTelemetry distributed tracing alongside structured logging for end-to-end visibility.
  • Configure service-level objectives (SLOs) and dashboards to monitor latency, failure rates, and retry behaviour.
  • Implement automated alerts for abnormal timeout trends before customer impact becomes significant.
  • Introduce circuit breakers and bulkheads where dependency behaviour or traffic patterns justify additional protection.
  • Perform controlled fault-injection testing to validate resilience under production-like failure scenarios.
