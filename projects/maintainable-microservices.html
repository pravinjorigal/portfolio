Building Maintainable Spring Boot Services

Developed modular Spring Boot microservices with clear boundaries between API, business, data, and external-integration concerns—making complex enterprise features easier to change and support.

Business context

A digital insurance platform contained services for registration, policy management, documents, authentication, and enterprise integrations. Requirements evolved continually, but services still needed to remain understandable, testable, and safe to extend.

The challenge

As databases, identity systems, messaging, and business services accumulate, tightly coupled code becomes expensive to change and difficult to troubleshoot. The objective was to deliver features while preserving clear responsibilities and API compatibility.

My contribution

I developed REST APIs and business logic, integrated enterprise systems, supported testing and deployment, reviewed code, and worked closely with frontend, QA, business, and DevOps partners to deliver production-ready features.

Architectural approach

Controllers handled input validation and responses; service classes held business rules; repositories encapsulated data access. External systems such as identity providers and messaging were isolated behind service interfaces, keeping the core business layer less dependent on implementation details.

This made new feature work, testing, and production investigation more predictable: teams could isolate whether an issue lived in request handling, business logic, data access, or a downstream integration.

Engineering decisions

  • Layered architecture: clearer ownership and lower coupling.
  • Dependency injection: improved testability and made implementation changes safer.
  • REST: fit synchronous customer-facing workflows with immediate responses.
  • Modular integration boundaries: contained the effects of external-system changes.

Outcome

The approach improved readability and onboarding, reduced the cost of enhancements, and made code reviews and incident investigation more effective. It supported dependable enterprise delivery without chasing unnecessary complexity.

What I would improve today

For a new service, I would add contract testing, Testcontainers integration coverage, Micrometer metrics, OpenTelemetry tracing, resilience patterns, automated health checks, and Java 21 virtual threads where blocking workloads justify them.

Key lesson

Enterprise software is judged over years, not a sprint. Clear boundaries and simple, consistent patterns are a delivery advantage—not just a code-style preference.

Recruiter summary

Established layered, testable Spring Boot service architecture across a multi-project insurance platform, co-leading a 4–5 engineer offshore team and making feature delivery, code review, and incident response measurably faster.
